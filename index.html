MISSING-PERSON FILE · RD-15

Rachel Never Came Back

Rachel was playing near a hawker centre with her friend Lily when she became unwell. Soon afterwards, she disappeared from the playground. A passer-by, Billy, reported seeing a girl with a pink bow and a hooded driver near a black private car.

The police immediately began a search. Rachel was later found safe in the stopped vehicle and received medical attention. Your team is reviewing the evidence to establish who deliberately helped take her away without her guardian’s permission.

Your task: Identify the three people who knowingly cooperated, distinguish their roles and identify the vehicle involved.

Investigation rules

This is a fictional case. Exactly three of the four suspects knowingly cooperated in taking Rachel away without permission. Confirmed recordings, identities, message records and examination findings are accurate. No account impersonation, substituted objects or other accomplices were involved.

For the grid, record each suspect’s location at 3.10 p.m. and the associated item. Each location and item must be used once. A costume or an ordinary possession alone does not establish guilt.

Adults and emergency services handle the rescue and medical care. No substance, dose or harmful method is described or required to solve the case.

Connect the Evidence

Pin 01 · 3.10 p.m. · Four locations

Synchronised footage placed Lily at the playground bench with Rachel’s water bottle, Mei Mei near the swings using her mobile phone, the pirate-costume seller at his hawker-centre stall holding a toy bottle, and the hooded driver in the car park holding his car keys.

Pin 02 · The water bottle

An examination found harmful contamination in Rachel’s remaining water. The bottle had been confirmed uncontaminated when her guardian handed it over at 3.00 p.m. Continuous footage then showed Lily as the only person to handle it apart from Rachel before it was secured for examination. The sealed evidence record ruled out later substitution.

Pin 03 · The message agreement

Verified messages sent before Rachel became unwell recorded a deliberate agreement. Lily wrote that Rachel’s guardian had not given permission and asked Mei Mei and the driver to help take her away anyway. Mei Mei replied that she would watch the path; the driver agreed to collect them. Investigators confirmed who personally sent each message and that it was a genuine plan, not a performance or misunderstanding.

Pin 04 · The pink bow

Continuous footage identified Mei Mei as the girl in the pink bow. At 3.13 p.m. she checked the path, signalled to the driver and then accompanied Rachel to the car. This matched her agreed role in the verified messages.

Pin 05 · The car’s occupants

Clear footage identified Lily, Mei Mei and Rachel boarding the same black private car at 3.14 p.m. The hooded driver was at the wheel. The number plate and occupants matched the vehicle later stopped by police.

Pin 06 · The driver’s knowledge

The driver’s verified reply acknowledged that Rachel’s guardian had not authorised the journey. This establishes knowing participation, rather than an innocent lift. His family relationship with Mei Mei is not the basis for that conclusion.

Pin 07 · The seller’s record

Continuous stall footage placed the pirate-costume seller at his stall from 3.00 to 3.20 p.m. The supposedly suspicious bottle was a sealed toy prop; examination established it was not the recovered harmful-substance container. No confirmed contact linked him to the plan.

Pin 08 · Billy’s observation

Billy saw a girl with a pink bow and a hooded driver near a black car. His report prompted the search, but the clearer recordings and identity checks established who they were. He could not see the contents of the rubbish bag.

Pin 09 · The black bag

An examination found ordinary packaging in the recovered rubbish bag, with no evidence connecting it to moving Rachel. Owning other black bags does not prove a link. Rachel was recorded entering the car openly, not being placed in a bag.

Pin 10 · The missed lesson

Mei Mei was expected at a 5.00 p.m. dance lesson. Attendance at a later lesson could not establish where she was at 3.10 p.m. Her earlier recorded actions and verified messages are the relevant evidence.

Pin 11 · Rachel found safe

At 3.18 p.m., police stopped the car. Rachel was inside with Lily, Mei Mei and the hooded driver. She received immediate medical attention and was reunited with her guardian. The rescue confirms the vehicle identity; the messages and recordings establish each person’s role.

Teacher Case Solution

Lily, Mei Mei and the hooded driver · Black private car

The verified messages establish a genuine agreement made with knowledge that Rachel’s guardian had not authorised the journey. Lily initiated the plan; Mei Mei agreed to watch the path and was recorded signalling; the driver acknowledged the lack of permission and drove the car. The recordings and vehicle check confirm the same group. Under the exactly-three rule, this is the unique responsible set.

The water evidence identifies a serious safety concern and a sole-handling interval, but the puzzle does not require a chemical name or a poisoning method. The cooperation is independently established by the messages and recorded actions.

The pirate-costume seller was at his stall and his bottle was a toy. A later dance lesson, the colour of a bag and the driver’s family relationship are not proof of participation.

Rachel was found safe in the car and received medical attention. The rubbish-bag theory was not supported and has been removed.
